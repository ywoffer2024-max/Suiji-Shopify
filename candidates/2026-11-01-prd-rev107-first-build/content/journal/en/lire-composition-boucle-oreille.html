The short answer

Read an earring component by component: decorative body, post or hook, back, solder/joints, stones or adhesives, then every coating. A single line such as “titanium earrings” or “gold coloured metal” cannot tell you what remains in contact with the ear.

Component map

Component Information needed Why it matters
Post/hook exact material, grade, dimensions passes through or rests against the ear
Back material, shape, size pressure and prolonged contact
Body/hoop base metal, solid/hollow construction mass and contact area
Solder/joint material or manufacturing statement may differ from body
Decoration stone, glass, resin, adhesive weight, care and exposure
Finish plating, PVD, anodising, lacquer hides or changes the base surface

Use SUIJI materials by component and care guidance to separate skin-contact components, decorative materials and finishes, then confirm them for the selected piece.

If the exact part or material is unclear, you can ask SUIJI a specific product question before placing an order.

Translate vague language into questions

  • “Hypoallergenic” → which parts, composition and evidence?
  • “Nickel free” → no intentional nickel, composition limit or release test?
  • “Surgical steel” → which grade?
  • “Titanium” → which grade, standard and certificate?
  • “Gold” or “gold coloured” → solid alloy, plating, PVD or colour only?
  • “925” → which components are sterling silver and what are the remaining metals?

Read evidence in layers

  1. Product description identifies every component.
  2. Material certificate identifies a material batch.
  3. Traceability connects the batch to a component.
  4. Finished-product tests address applicable hazards.
  5. Quality control shows how production is checked.

These documents answer different questions. A mill certificate is not a nickel-release test; a test on the post is not automatically a test on the back.

Example of a useful specification

Post: [exact material/grade]. Back: [exact material]. Main body: [exact material and construction]. Surface: [process and colour]. Weight: [x g per earring]. Dimensions: [x × y mm]. Evidence: [document type, date, sample/lot and scope].

Do not fill this template with assumptions. Unknown fields should remain pending until approved product data exists.

Buying checklist

  • [ ] every contact component is identified;
  • [ ] colour is separated from material;
  • [ ] post/back are not hidden under “main material”;
  • [ ] weight and dimensions use clear units;
  • [ ] evidence scope and limitations are shown;
  • [ ] care instructions match coatings/stones;
  • [ ] seller identity and returns are visible.

Make a contact map of the earring

The most useful composition is the one for surfaces that actually touch the ear. Photograph or sketch the earring from the front, side and back, then assign a material to the post, back, body, solder, connecting ring and decoration.

This exposes over-broad descriptions. An earring may have a sterling-silver body, steel post, different solder and a separately supplied back. Calling the whole object “925 silver” leaves several contact areas unanswered.

For a hoop, include the segment that passes through the hole and the hinge. For a leverback, separate the moving wire, lever and decorative body. For a clip, identify both pinching surfaces and any pad.

Colour is not composition

Gold, silver, rose gold and black may describe appearance rather than the base material. Translate each colour into a process question: solid alloy, plating, PVD, anodising, lacquer or another finish?

Then ask which zones are treated. A gold-coloured body may have an uncoated post or back. A titanium body may receive a deposited coating whose composition and wear need separate evidence. Cleaning instructions should match the weakest component, not the most durable metal named in the title.

Acceptable, partial and insufficient answers

Seller answer Assessment Reason
“Post and back: ASTM F136 titanium; batch certificate available” precise material, components, specification and traceability are connected
“Titanium earring, metal back” partial rear contact component remains unknown
“Premium hypoallergenic” insufficient no composition or evidence
“Sterling silver” partial scope, alloying metals, post and solder are unclear
“Gold PVD” insufficient alone base metal and coated areas are missing

A partial answer is the next question, not automatic proof of a bad product. Contradictory answers between the page, packaging and customer service are more serious and should be resolved before wear.

Material certificates and finished-product tests

A mill certificate describes a material batch; a product test measures a defined sample under a method. Neither should be stretched beyond its scope.

A certificate for titanium bar stock can support the grade of a traceable post. It does not test a coating, solder joint or separately sourced closure. An EN 1811 nickel-release report can support the tested components and sample; it does not establish every future lot or the complete chemical composition.

Build evidence in layers: component description, material document, production traceability, relevant finished-product test and ongoing quality control.

Keep a copy of what was promised

A product page can change after your order. Save the listing, date, selected variant and any customer-service response with the invoice.

This record helps compare purchases, report inconsistent information and show a doctor or dermatologist the advertised composition if a reaction occurs. It does not prove that the claim was correct, but it documents what the seller represented at the time of sale.

A reusable specification template

Post: [exact material/grade]. Back: [exact material]. Body: [material and construction]. Joint/solder: [material if present]. Surface: [process, colour and covered areas]. Decoration: [stone/resin/glue where relevant]. Weight: [g per earring]. Dimensions: [mm]. Evidence: [document, date, sample or lot, scope]. Intended use: [healed lobe/initial piercing/occasional wear].

Leave unknown fields open rather than filling them with assumptions. A publishable product page should replace each bracket with approved product data or state the limitation clearly.

What if the seller cannot answer?

Treat the missing field as uncertainty and adjust the intended use. A decorative earring for brief wear on a healed lobe and initial jewellery placed in a healing wound do not tolerate the same information gap.

Ask one precise follow-up and keep the reply. If the post or back remains unidentified, do not infer its material from the front. If a seller cannot connect a certificate to the product, describe the evidence as unverified rather than calling it false or valid.

Recheck the specification when a variant changes

Different colours and closures can use different components. A silver-coloured and gold-coloured option may not share the same coating, while a clip conversion can introduce new contact materials. Save and verify data for the exact variant, not only the parent product.

When a product is sold singly and as a pair, also verify whether both configurations include the same back and accessories.

Use the same component names on the product page, packaging, invoice and customer-service reply so the evidence remains traceable after purchase.

FAQ

Does “titanium earrings” mean the back is titanium?

Not necessarily. The product page should say which components the claim covers.

Is “nickel free” a full composition?

No. It describes one condition or claim, not all materials.

Does a certificate prove the finished earring?

Only to the extent that its scope and traceability match the product. Additional parts and processes remain separate.

Why list weight per earring?

It makes symmetric and asymmetric products comparable and avoids ambiguity in pair weight.

Reading a SUIJI listing

SUIJI bases are titanium. Where TC4 is specified, it describes the documented metalwork. A champagne or blue finish is an appearance choice, not a claim that the piece is gold or silver. Ornaments, stones, posts and backs remain separate components. Moissanite describes only stones identified as such; a colored ornament is not automatically moissanite. Read the included-parts list and the exact option.
